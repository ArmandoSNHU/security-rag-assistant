# GDPR — General Data Protection Regulation

The GDPR is the European Union's data-protection law. It governs how organizations collect, store, and use the personal data of people in the EU, and it applies to organizations outside the EU when they process that data.

## Core principles

Processing of personal data must be lawful, fair, and transparent. Data should be collected for specified purposes (purpose limitation), kept to what is necessary (data minimization), accurate, retained no longer than needed (storage limitation), and protected by appropriate security (integrity and confidentiality). Organizations must also be able to demonstrate compliance (accountability).

## Rights and obligations

Individuals have rights including access to their data, rectification, erasure ("right to be forgotten"), restriction, portability, and objection. A lawful basis — such as consent, contract, legal obligation, or legitimate interest — is required before processing.

## Breach notification

When a personal-data breach is likely to pose a risk to individuals, the controller must notify its supervisory authority without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours of becoming aware of it. If the risk to individuals is high, the affected individuals must be informed as well. Penalties for serious violations can reach the higher of a set monetary ceiling or a percentage of global annual turnover, which is why breach handling and record-keeping are treated as compliance priorities.
